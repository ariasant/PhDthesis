%-------------------------------------------------------------------- abstract
\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Observational evidence shows that the Milky Way has followed a hierarchical formation process,
progressively accreting matter from smaller, in-falling satellite galaxies. The observable remnants of
this turbulent process are stars which were tidally-disrupted from the progenitor galaxies and that
are now orbiting within the stellar halo, a Galactic structure surrounding the disk and bulge of the Milky Way. 
The hierarchical structure formation process and the emergence of the stellar halo are predicted by
the standard cosmological model and reproduced by current cosmological simulations of galaxy formation. \\

The scope of this thesis is to develop machine learning models to connect the theoretical understanding of 
galaxy formation encoded in cosmological simulations to the properties of stars 
in the stellar halo. These models are tested on the \texttt{ARTEMIS} and \texttt{Auriga} suites of
cosmological zoom-in simulations and, ultimately, applied to the Milky Way. \\

At first, I derive the a data-driven model to separate accreted stars from background stars by 
comparing several machine learning algorithms and sets of stellar properties. I find that an artificial neural
network trained on the chrono-chemo-dynamical properties of stars can recover accreted stars at 
higher retrieval ($>90\%$) and purity ($\sim60\%$) rates than observationally-motivated selection cuts in kinematic and 
chemical space.   \\

Building on this classification, I introduce a new methodology to automatically fine-tune the 
parameters of the \texttt{HDBSCAN} clustering algorithm, which is used to group accreted stars 
into the progenitor satellite systems in which they formed. Contrary to models without any
fine-tuning, the optimised \texttt{HDBSCAN} algorithm does not return spurious clusters and can detect substructures 
from merging events up to $z\sim3$ for accreted-only stellar halos, with the horizon falling to $z\sim1$ 
when accounting for in-situ contamination. \\

Once stars are grouped into the galaxies where they formed, I pioneer the application of simulation-based inference 
to characterize the properties of each merger event. I introduce GalactiKit, a framework to recover the
infall time, stellar mass, halo mass, and merger mass ratio of a progenitor galaxy at the time of accretion
from the present-day chemo-dynamical properties of its debris. I show that kinematics alone can be used to
constrain the lookback time to the merger event, while chemical information is essential for the mass estimates. \\

Finally, I apply the GalactiKit framework to a sample of pre-identified accreted stars in the Milky Way, 
producing the largest, self-consistent reconstruction of our Galaxy's assembly history to date. I predict that
the Gaia-Enceladus Sausage ($\sim43\%$) and Sagittarius ($\sim31\%$) dwarf galaxies comprise the majority of the accreted stellar content
of the Milky Way, which amounts to a total mass of $2.2^{+1.1}_{-0.6} \times 10^{9}~\rm{M_{\sun}}$. 
I also provide an estimate of the growth of the total mass of the
Galactic halo between mergers between $z\sim5$ and $z\sim1$ inferring a total mass of the Milky Way of $5.9^{+1.4}_{-1.1} \times 10^{11}~\rm{M_{\sun}}$ 
after the accretion of Sagittarius.  \\

Together, these methods constitute a novel, scalable framework for connecting present-day stellar populations 
to the merger histories that produced them. The next steps involve integrating these individual components 
into a single, end-to-end pipeline for reconstructing the Milky Way's assembly history, and, 
prospectively, extending this framework to other galaxies observed with upcoming large-scale surveys.

\chapter*{Publications}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Publications}

During the course of the preparation of this thesis, the work within
Chapters~\ref{chap:chapter2}, \ref{chap:chapter3}, \ref{chap:chapter4},
and~\ref{chap:chapter5} has been presented in the following jointly authored
publications:

\bigskip

\noindent 1 \textit{`Applying machine learning to Galactic Archaeology: how well can we
recover the origin of stars in Milky Way-like galaxies?'}\\
\textbf{Sante, A.,} Font, A. S., Ortega-Martorell, S., Olier, I.,
\& McCarthy, I. G., \href{https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stae1398}{MNRAS, Volume~531, Issue~4}.

\bigskip

\noindent 2 \textit{`Optimized HDBSCAN clustering for reconstructing the merger history
of the Milky Way: applications and limitations'}\\
\textbf{Sante, A.,} Font, A. S., Mistry, I., Ortega-Martorell, S., \& Olier, I.,
\href{https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stag503}{MNRAS, Volume~547, Issue~4}.

\bigskip

\noindent 3 \textit{`GalactiKit: reconstructing mergers from $z=0$ debris using
simulation-based inference in Auriga'}\\
\textbf{Sante, A.,} Kawata, D., Font, A. S., \& Grand, R. J. J.,
\href{https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/staf1343}{MNRAS, Volume~542, Issue~3}.

\bigskip

\noindent 4 \textit{`A simulation-based inference of the Milky Way merger history'}\\
\textbf{Sante, A.,} Font, A. S., Kawata, D., Makinen, T. L., \& Grand, R. J. J.,
\href{https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stag1162}{MNRAS, Volume 550, Issue 2}.

\bigskip
\bigskip

Publication~1 is presented in Chapter~\ref{chap:chapter2}. It describes
several machine learning models trained on the
\texttt{ARTEMIS} cosmological zoom-in simulations
to separate accreted from in-situ
stars in Milky Way-mass galaxies. The analysis was carried out by
A.~Sante with major scientific contributions from A.~S.~Font and I.~G. McCarthy. 
Ortega-Martorell, S. and Olier, I. reviewed and supported the implementation
of the machine learning models. 

\bigskip

Publication~2 is presented in Chapter~\ref{chap:chapter3}. It develops
a novel parameter optimisation pipeline for the \texttt{HDBSCAN} clustering algorithm,
testing its effectiveness for recovering accreted substructures under 
different regimes of contamination of in-situ stars.
The analysis was carried out by A.~Sante and Mistry, I. with major scientific
contributions from all the co-authors.

\bigskip

Publication~3 is presented in Chapter~\ref{chap:chapter4}. It describes
GalactiKit, a simulation-based inference framework for estimating the infall
time, stellar mass, halo mass and halo mass merger ratio of disrupted progenitors from
the properties of their $z=0$ debris in the Auriga simulations. The analysis
was carried out by A.~Sante with major scientific contributions from the
co-authors.

\bigskip

Publication~4 is presented in Chapter~\ref{chap:chapter5}. It applies 
GalactiKit to a local sample of accreted stars in the Milky Way,
inferring the accretion times and masses of several known merger events
and placing constraints on the growth of the Galactic halo. The analysis
was carried out by A.~Sante with the support of T.~L.~Makinen for the 
implementation of the data compression pipeline, and A.~S.~Font, D.~Kawata, and
R.~J.~J.~Grand for the scientific context and application of the models.

\bigskip

In addition to the publications forming the corpus of this thesis, I worked
on the analysis of the simulation data for the comparison between disc and
``Splash'' stellar populations in \citet{Kisku2025}.
