\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{chap:conclusions}

The work in this thesis represents an effort in advancing the methodologies for 
reconstructing the assembly history of the Milky Way by improving and, in some cases,
pioneering the application of state-of-the-art machine learning methods on
cosmological hydrodynamical simulations.  
Moving towards a fully data-driven reconstruction of the Galaxy merger history is necessary to
harness the information in the large amount of data from modern surveys to extract
insights and constrain our understanding of Galaxy Formation.   
Each chapter of this thesis adresses one core challenge in this process. The main conclusions
of the study are summarised below.

\bigskip

In Chapter~\ref{chap:chapter2} I propose a data-driven identification of accreted stars showing that
the choice of machine learning algorithm is indifferent for the task. The discriminant
is the choice of the parameter space in which the classification is performed. The full chrono-chemo-dynamical
information of the stellar content allows for the optimal separation between the accreted and in-situ 
populations (purity $\sim60\%$ and completeness $\sim90\%$); however, the photometric and kinematic
properties of stars also provide a ground of similar quality for the separation. This is
particularly interesting as accurate chemical abundances are harder to obtain because they require
high-resolution spectroscopic surveys and accurate spectral fitting models.
The data-driven classification models outperform selection-cut based methods
when applied to star particles from solar neighbourhood regions in the simulations, which shows that the application of these
methods can have an immediate impact in the identification of accreted stars in the Milky Way.
However, these conclusions rely on cosmological simulations, which do not resolve 
single stars but approximate entire stellar populations as single particles. 
This inevitably leads to differences in the chemo-dynamical distributions of stars in the
simulations compared to the one observed in the Milky Way. For instance, stars belonging to a single stellar populations 
appear in different stages of stellar evolution and, as such, present a variance in their photometric and chemical abundances 
which is ignored in the smoothed-particle hydrodynamics approximation in simulations.


Following the identification of accreted stars, Chapter~\ref{chap:chapter3} presents the first data-driven optimisation
of the parameters of the \texttt{HDBSCAN} algorithm, which is popularly used for the identification of accreted substructure 
in the Milky Way. I show that tuning the \texttt{HDBSCAN} parameters to maximise the DBCV metric, an intrinsic 
metric assessing the intra-cluster density, leads to a configuration of the model which is also optimal
in terms of the purity and completeness of the single clusters. This is a promising result as clustering
with arbitrary parameter initialisation have been shown to return clusters dominated by
in-situ stars or stars from multiple progenitors \citep{Thomas2025}. 
The developed of a parameter optimisation pipeline provides, instead, a way of automatically tuning these models
to maximise cluster purity. When applied to a pure samples of accreted stars, the resulting \texttt{HDBSCAN} model is able to retrieve
merger events up to $z\sim3$, while in the presence of in-situ stars, this threshold lowers to $z\sim1$ due to phase-mixing 
effects and the similar chemical abundances between the different building blocks of the galaxy.

In Chapter~\ref{chap:chapter4} I develop GalactiKit, a pioneering application of simulation-based inference for the prediction of the infall time,
stellar and halo mass, and halo mass merger ratio of the disrupted progenitors of the Milky Way from the chemo-dynamical properties
of their debris at $z=0$. This methodology leverages the information encoded in the \texttt{Auriga} cosmological hydrodynamical simulations to create
a data-driven mapping between galaxy and stellar properties.
I show that the total energy and angular momentum of stars can constrain the
lookback infall time of progenitors, a known result for intact satellites \citep{Rocha2012} and now extended to phase-mixed substructures.
Similarly, I show that stellar chemical abundances are also tracers of the satellite accretion time, due to the effect that this 
time scale has on the chemical evolution of the system, and stellar mass, as expected from
the stellar mass-halo mass relation \citep{Kirby2013}. Combining the kinematic and chemical properties of debris
provides the largest amount of information in reconstructing the properties of a satellite, with the kinematic information improving
the estimate of the stellar and halo mass estimates of low-mass, high-metallicity systems.

Finally, Chapter~\ref{chap:chapter5} applies GalactiKit to observed accreted substructures in the Milky Way. 
A central challenge is that simulation-trained models are applied to observations drawn from a different 
distribution. I address this by modelling the simulation-to-observation discrepancy in stellar properties as a 
stochastic ``calibration noise'', augmenting the \texttt{Auriga} training set with draws from this distribution, 
and marginalising the residual uncertainty through the Fishnets data-compression model. 
By applying this improved version of GalactiKit, I have obtained the largest,
self-consistent reconstruction of the assembly history of the Milky Way. I predict that the accreted stellar content in out Galaxy
amounts to $2.2^{+1.1}_{-0.6} \times 10^{9}~\rm{M_{\sun}}$, most of which was accreted between $z\sim2$ and $z\sim1$. The majority 
of this mass comes from the accretion of the GES and Sagittarius dwarf galaxies, contributing 0.43 and 0.30 of the total accreted
stellar content, respectively. Combining the predictions on the halo mass and halo mass merger ratio of the Milky Way progenitors, 
I estimated the growth of the Milky Way dark matter halo predicting it is not fully explained by the current census of mergers, hence
hinting to potential undetected events, or early epoch of dominated by the accretion of dark matter-only systems.  

\bigskip

While providing predictions on the Milky Way assembly history, the work in Chapter~\ref{chap:chapter5} relies on
the identification of accreted substrctures determined using out-of-the-box
clustering algorithms on samples of accreted stars selected through hard selection cuts. 
Despite the work in this thesis presents data-driven methodologies for the individual steps involved in 
a Galactic Archaelology study, the development of a fully data-driven, end-to-end pipeline remain challenging.
The current clustering methodology developed in Chapter~\ref{chap:chapter3} requires complete and pure samples of accreted
stars to retrieve mergers occurring before $z\sim1$, while the classification model developed in Chapter~\ref{chap:chapter3}
retrieves accreted samples with 0.60 purity
at the fiducial classification threshold. Moreover, the absence of a domain adaptation technique addressing the 
difference between the simulated and observed stellar properties, makes the performance of the classifier on observational data uncertain. 

Therefore, the most imminent work direction is the validation of the current clustering methodolgy in case where the 
contamination of in-situ stars is significant to probe the relationship between the latest retrivable
redshift of accretion and the fraction of in-situ stars in the accreted sample. This would inform on 
whether better classification models are required to better probe the assembly history of the Galaxy. 
The separation between accreted and in-situ stars can be improved using context-aware architectures, 
such as attention or graph neural networks, which encoding each star's galactic context as an 
embedding alongside its stellar properties. 
Regardless of architecture, a domain-adaptation term is necessary to ensure 
robust performance on observational data.

With the advent of a new era of large-scale stellar surveys, the development of such an end-to-end data-driven pipeline becomes a priority.
The fourth data release of Gaia, expected in December 2026 \citep{GaiaCollaboration2016a}, 
will exploit the full $\sim$10-year operational baseline to deliver proper motions for over 2 billion stars with a precision up to $\sim$0.010~mas~yr$^{-1}$, 
as well as yielding medium-resolution chemical abundances for more than 5 million stars.
Complementing Gaia's legacy, a suite of large ground-based spectroscopic surveys is simultaneously expanding the chemical dimensionality of stellar catalogues.
The fourth data release of GALAH \citep{Buder2025} has already provided up to 31 high-resolution, individual elemental abundances for $\sim$827\,000 solar-neighbourhood stars, while 
the WEAVE survey \citep{Dalton2014} will provide spectroscopy for $\sim$5 million targets across the northern sky. 
Furthermore, the DESI Milky Way Survey \citep{Cooper2023} is expected to obtain accurate spectroscopy for $\sim$6.6 million stars in the Galactic thick disc and inner stellar halo, while the
Milky Way Mapper Survey already provides a dense and continuouse mapping with BOSS low and APOGEE medium-resolution measurements for over 4 million sources at low Galactic latitudes \citep{Meszaros2025}.  
Together, these surveys will provide accurate chemo-dynamical measurements for tens of millions of stars at an unprecedented scale, 
forming the observational foundation on which the data-driven methods can be exploit to constrain the formation history of the Milky Way. 

Looking further ahead, a new generation of wide-field space observatories will broaden the scope of 
Galactic Archaeology from a study of one galaxy into a comparative, statistical science.
The Vera~C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time \citep[LSST;][]{Ivezic2008}, 
now underway, will map $\sim$10 billion stars to a coadded depth of $r\sim$27.5~mag over $\sim$18\,000~deg$^{2}$, 
with a surface brightness limit of $\sim$30--31~mag~arcsec$^{-2}$ that will prove transformative 
for detecting stellar streams and diffuse halo substructure; 
its 10-year proper motion baseline extends kinematic mapping to $r\lesssim24$, 
well beyond the magnitude completeness of Gaia \citep{Bonaca2025}.
On Local Group scales, the Euclid satellite \citep{Euclid2025}, which is already operational, will 
resolve individual stars in M31 and its dwarf satellites with its VIS imager reaching 
a surface brightness of $\sim$29--31~mag~arcsec$^{-2}$ providing detailed photometry  
for dozens of Local Group galaxies.
The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope \citep{Roman}
will provide complementary deep near-infrared colour-magnitude diagrams 
for resolved stellar populations throughout the Local Group at 0.11~arcsec~pixel$^{-1}$.
Further ahead, the ARRAKIHS satellite \citep{Guzman2022}, with a launch planned for $\sim$2030,
will conduct the first dedicated survey of the ultra-low surface brightness stellar halos, tidal streams, and satellite populations 
of $\sim$75--80 nearby Milky Way-mass galaxies, reaching $\gtrsim$30~mag~arcsec$^{-2}$.

Applied to the resolved stellar populations of Local Group galaxies via Euclid 
and Roman, or to the halo profiles of Milky Way-mass analogues via ARRAKIHS, 
the data-driven methods of this thesis offer a route to the first comparative 
census of galaxy assembly histories, anchoring the Milky Way's formation within 
the broader cosmological framework of hierarchical structure formation.
